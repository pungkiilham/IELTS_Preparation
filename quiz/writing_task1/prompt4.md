## Band 6 Example (150-165 words)

The diagram illustrates the chocolate manufacturing process, from raw cocoa beans to finished chocolate bars.

Initially, cocoa beans undergo cleaning and roasting to remove impurities and enhance flavour. The roasted beans are then ground into small particles, which are mixed with sugar before being pressed. This pressing process separates the cocoa butter from the solid remnants.

The extracted cocoa butter is subsequently combined with other ingredients and undergoes conching—a smoothing treatment that refines the texture. Finally, the mixture is moulded into shape and left to cool, producing the chocolate bars available in stores.

Overall, the process involves eight distinct stages, transforming raw cocoa beans into finished products through a series of heating, grinding, and mixing procedures.

## Band 9 Example (180-200 words)

The diagram presents the complete production process for manufactured chocolate, beginning with raw cocoa beans and concluding with finished chocolate bars ready for consumption.

The process commences with the cleaning and roasting of cocoa beans, which serves to eliminate impurities and develop the characteristic chocolate flavour profile. Subsequently, the roasted beans are ground into a fine paste, which is combined with sugar before being subjected to high-pressure pressing. This critical step effectively separates the cocoa butter—a valuable fat component—from the remaining solid matter.

The extracted cocoa butter is then recombined with the other ingredients, underwent a process known as conching, during which the mixture is continuously kneaded at elevated temperatures to achieve a smooth, velvety texture and eliminate any residual bitterness. Following this refinement stage, the homogeneous mixture is poured into moulds and allowed to solidify through cooling.

In summary, chocolate production encompasses eight sequential stages, progressing from initial bean preparation through to the final moulding phase, with the conversion rate of cocoa butter back into the main mixture representing a particularly distinctive characteristic of this manufacturing process.
