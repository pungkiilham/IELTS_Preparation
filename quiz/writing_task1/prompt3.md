## Band 6 Example (145-155 words)

The table illustrates the primary motivations for studying among students in a particular nation during two years: 2000 and 2020.

In 2000, career advancement was the leading reason at 40%, followed by personal interest at 30%. Higher income accounted for 20%, while social reasons represented only 10%. By 2020, career advancement had grown significantly to 55%, making it the dominant factor. In contrast, personal interest dropped to 25%, and both higher income and social reasons declined to 15% and 5% respectively.

Overall, career advancement became the overwhelming motive for study over the two decades, while all other reasons decreased in popularity.

## Band 9 Example (170-190 words)

The table compares the main reasons why students pursued education in one country in 2000 versus 2020.

In 2000, career advancement led at 40%, followed by personal interest at 30%, higher income at 20%, and social reasons trailing at just 10%. Twenty years later, career advancement surged to 55%—an increase of 15 percentage points—becoming the dominant driver. Meanwhile, personal interest fell to 25%, higher income declined to 15%, and social reasons halved to 5%.

The most notable shift is the growing importance of career advancement, which more than doubled in relative terms from comprising two-fifths to over half of all motivations. Conversely, personal interest and social reasons both diminished substantially over this period.

Overall, the data reveals a clear trend: student's motivations became increasingly centred on professional development rather than personal enrichment or social factors.
