## Band 6 Example (270 words)

There are differing views about whether schools should prioritize competition or cooperation in their teaching approach. Some argue that students should be trained to be competitive and pursue personal goals. Others believe that schools should focus on teaching teamwork and collaboration. In my opinion, while both skills are important, competition drives individual excellence.

Proponents of competitive education argue that in today's job market, students must stand out to succeed. With increasing population and competition for limited positions, individuals need specialized skills to distinguish themselves. For example, candidates who demonstrate exceptional abilities in fields like programming or finance are more likely to secure desirable positions at top companies. Therefore, schools should nurture individual talents and ambition.

However, supporters of cooperative learning emphasize that professional success often depends on teamwork. In most workplaces, employees must collaborate with colleagues, managers, and clients to achieve common goals. A software engineer, for instance, works daily with product managers, designers, and quality assurance teams. Without cooperation skills, even the most talented individual would struggle in such environments.

In my view, competition is more beneficial because it pushes students to develop expertise and stand out in competitive markets. While teamwork skills are useful, they can be learned naturally in professional settings, whereas specialized knowledge requires formal education.

## Band 7 Example (285 words)

There are differing views about whether schools should prioritize competition or cooperation in their teaching approach. Some argue that students should be trained to be competitive and pursue personal goals. Others believe that schools should focus on teaching teamwork and collaboration. In my opinion, while both skills are valuable, competition plays a more critical role in preparing students for future success.

Proponents of competitive education argue that in today's globalized job market, students must develop unique abilities to stand out among millions of graduates. For instance, candidates who demonstrate exceptional proficiency in fields like artificial intelligence or quantitative finance are significantly more likely to secure positions at leading technology companies such as Google, Meta, or Goldman Sachs. Furthermore, competitive environments encourage students to push beyond their limitations and strive for excellence, qualities that are highly valued by employers.

Conversely, supporters of cooperative learning emphasize that professional success in the modern workplace heavily depends on interpersonal skills. Research indicates that the average professional spends approximately 80% of their workday communicating with colleagues, managers, and clients. A software engineer, for example, must collaborate daily with product managers, designers, and quality assurance teams to deliver successful projects. Without effective cooperation skills, even the most talented individual would struggle to contribute meaningfully to their organization.

In my view, schools should prioritize competition because it drives innovation and personal growth. While teamwork skills can be developed through workplace experience, the foundational expertise required in specialized fields must be cultivated during education.

## Band 9 Example (300 words)

The debate over whether educational institutions should foster competition or cooperation has long divided educators and policymakers. While some argue that schools should prioritize developing students' competitive spirit and individual achievement, others maintain that teamwork and collaboration are essential life skills. This essay will examine both perspectives before offering a balanced assessment.

Advocates of competitive education assert that in an increasingly globalized job market, individual excellence is paramount. Empirical studies indicate that students who develop specialized expertise early in their academic careers enjoy significantly higher employment rates and starting salaries. For instance, graduates with advanced certifications in artificial intelligence or data science command starting salaries exceeding $120,000 in the United States technology sector. Furthermore, competitive environments motivate students to push beyond their limitations and achieve remarkable accomplishments that would otherwise remain undiscovered.

Conversely, proponents of collaborative learning argue that contemporary workplaces demand exceptional interpersonal competencies. Modern organizations operate through complex networks of specialists who must communicate effectively to deliver results. Research from the World Economic Forum suggests that teamwork and emotional intelligence rank among the most sought-after qualities in the twenty-first century. Without the ability to collaborate harmoniously, even the most capable professionals struggle to contribute meaningfully to their organizations.

In my assessment, the optimal educational approach combines competitive and cooperative elements. Schools should encourage students to develop specialized expertise while simultaneously fostering interpersonal skills through group projects and collaborative assignments.
