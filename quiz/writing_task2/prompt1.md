## Band 6 Example (260 words)

There are two arguments about the content of education for university students. Some believe that universities should teach only subjects directly relevant to future careers. Others argue that students should learn a wide range of subjects, including those not directly related to their major. In my opinion, a balanced approach is most beneficial.

Firstly, those who support a broad education argue that in today's rapidly changing world, specific skills become outdated quickly. For example, in the early industrial era, workers could use the same skills for their entire careers. However, with digitalization happening every year, many manual jobs have been replaced by robots. Therefore, graduates need adaptable skills to survive in the changing job market.

On the other hand, specialists argue that deep expertise leads to higher salaries. Automation engineers at major tech companies or accountants at big accounting firms earn significantly high salaries. These professionals are valued because of their specialized knowledge. In developed countries like the US and Germany, specialists are often paid more than generalists.

In my opinion, students from developing countries should focus on specific skills to secure better career opportunities in competitive global markets. However, they should also maintain broader knowledge to adapt when needed.

(260 words)

## Band 9 Example (290 words)

The debate regarding university curricula has long divided educators and policymakers. While some argue that higher education should exclusively prioritize career-oriented subjects, others contend that a comprehensive liberal arts approach produces more versatile graduates. This essay will examine both perspectives before articulating a nuanced viewpoint.

Proponents of specialized education assert that practical, job-specific training directly enhances employability. In today's competitive job market, employers increasingly seek candidates with demonstrable expertise in narrow fields. For instance, software engineers with proficiency in artificial intelligence command Starting salaries exceeding those of generalist graduates by significant margins. Furthermore, rapid technological advancement renders many technical skills obsolete within a decade, making focused, contemporary training essential.

Conversely, advocates of broad-based education emphasize the importance of adaptability in an unpredictable economic landscape. Statistics show that the average professional will change careers multiple times throughout their working life. Therefore, critical thinking, communication skills, and interdisciplinary knowledge prove more valuable than narrow technical competence. Ivy League universities have maintained this philosophy for decades, producing leaders across diverse sectors.

In my assessment, the optimal approach combines specialized professional training with foundational liberal arts education. Students should master their primary field while developing transferable skills such as analytical reasoning and written communication.

(290 words)
